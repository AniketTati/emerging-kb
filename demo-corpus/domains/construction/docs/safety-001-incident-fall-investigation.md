# SITE INCIDENT INVESTIGATION REPORT — DETAILED

**Site:** Acme Whitefield Datacentre Phase-2
**Incident date:** 22 May 2025 11:42 IST
**Investigation team:**

**Report date:** 1 June 2025

## 1. Investigation Method

The investigation used the "5 Whys" + "Bow-Tie" methodology. Steps:

(i) Site visit + scene reconstruction (23 May 2025)
(ii) Interviews with 8 witnesses + Mr. Kumar (interview conducted at hospital on 25 May 2025)
(iii) Document review — PPE issuance logs, scaffold inspection records, toolbox-briefing records, harness inspection records, weekly Job Safety Analysis (JSA) records
(iv) Photographic + video evidence review (site CCTV: 4 cameras covering the area)
(v) PPE inventory audit — physical check of all 142 workers' PPE
(vi) Independent expert consultation (Dr. Suresh Krishnan)
(vii) Cross-reference with industry incident databases (DGMS + Karnataka Labour Dept)

## 2. Reconstruction of the Incident

CCTV footage + witness statements establish the following sequence:

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| 09:18 | Mr. Kumar reported to site. Per attendance log + biometric. |
| 09:30 | Toolbox briefing conducted (~5 min). Mr. Kumar attended. |
| 09:45 | PPE issuance: helmet (Y), harness (N — see below), safety boots (Y), gloves (Y). |
| 10:18 | Mr. Kumar climbed to working level on Scaffold 18-E (East elevation, Grid 6). |
| 10:42 | Started masonry work + intermittent mortar batching. |
| 11:42 | Lost balance reaching for mortar — fell. |

**Critical finding from CCTV:** At 09:45, when PPE was issued, **the harness inventory at the PPE issue counter was depleted**. The issuing clerk (Mr. Suresh Pandey, PPE inventory clerk) noted in the manual register that "harness pending stock — worker to collect later". However, NO LATER WAS COLLECTED.

Mr. Kumar's harness was found on the ground at scene at 11:43 IST — confirming he was working without his harness at the time of the fall.

---

## 3. ⚠ CRITICAL REVISION OF ROOT CAUSE (vs Initial Report)

The initial 24-hour report (safety-001-incident-fall-initial, dated 22 May 2025) attributed the cause to **worker error** (failure to clip harness). After the detailed investigation, this attribution is **INCORRECT**.

The detailed investigation finds:

### Primary root cause: PPE non-availability + supervisory gap (system failure)

(i) **PPE not issued:** Harnesses were out of stock at the morning issue desk. Mr. Kumar did not receive a harness because no harness was available. This is a **provisioning failure**, not a worker decision.

(ii) **Supervision gap:** The site supervisor (Mr. Rajesh Pal, Site Foreman) did not check Mr. Kumar's PPE before allowing him onto the scaffold. Per the standard Job Safety Analysis (JSA), the supervisor MUST verify harness donning before any height work begins. This check did not happen.

(iii) **Toolbox briefing inadequate:** The morning toolbox briefing did NOT include verification that all workers proceeding to height work had harnesses. The briefing was generic.

(iv) **No alternative protection:** Mr. Kumar should have been DENIED access to the scaffold without a harness. Alternative protection (e.g., guardrail) was not in place because the scaffold did not have an integrated rail at the working face.

### Contributing factors

(v) PPE inventory management: The PPE inventory had been depleted for 2 days before the incident. The PPE clerk had requested replenishment but it had not been actioned by procurement (Mahalaxmi's HQ logistics).

(vi) Audit gap: The most recent monthly PPE audit (April 2025) had NOT identified the harness shortage as a critical risk.

(vii) Cultural factor: There appears to be a tacit acceptance among supervisors that workers will "make do" if PPE is short — a culture inconsistent with zero-tolerance height-work safety.

### What the worker did NOT do wrong

Mr. Kumar:
- Attended the toolbox briefing
- Wore his helmet + boots correctly
- Did not violate any specific instruction
- Was working under direct supervision (which failed to verify his PPE)

**Conclusion: The initial "worker error" hypothesis is REJECTED.** The proximate cause was the failure of the PPE provisioning + supervision system, not Mr. Kumar's action.

## 5. Compensation Determination

(i) Statutory Workmen Compensation: ~INR 6.18 lakh (based on his monthly wage of INR 18,400 + duration of disability of 6-8 weeks per medical certificate)

(ii) Ex-gratia from Mahalaxmi (above statutory): **INR 12.00 lakh** in recognition that the company's system failure caused the incident. Payment in two tranches: INR 6 lakh on hospital discharge + INR 6 lakh on RTW.

(iii) Medical treatment costs: 100% Mahalaxmi (via WC insurance with HDFC ERGO).

(iv) Family support: Travel + accommodation for spouse and son in Bangalore till RTW — fully reimbursed.

(v) RTW transition: Light-duty role for 4-8 weeks post-RTW, full pay continuing.

Total estimated cost to Mahalaxmi: ~INR 20-22 lakh + insurance subrogation.

## 7. Recommendations + Corrective Actions

This investigation recommends the following corrective actions, fully documented in safety-001-incident-fall-corrective:

(i) Immediate replenishment of PPE inventory + introduction of buffer stock = 50% safety margin (every PPE item)
(ii) Mandatory PPE verification by supervisor + recorded in JSA log before height work
(iii) Spot-checks by Safety Officer at start of every shift
(iv) Disciplinary action: written warning + safety training for Mr. Rajesh Pal (Site Foreman)
(v) PIP for Safety Officer (Mr. Pradeep Bhargava)
(vi) Procurement-HQ process review + introduction of automatic re-order triggers
(vii) Site-wide safety culture review with workshops led by Dr. Suresh Krishnan
(viii) Implementation of digital PPE-tracking (RFID tags on PPE items) within 6 months

## 2. Overall Audit Opinion

**Satisfactory.** The Company's operations are reasonably well-controlled given its early-stage NBFC status. We identified **3 high-priority observations** and **4 medium-priority observations**.

Key strengths:
- Loan origination process is well-documented + approved by independent risk committee
- KYC + AML procedures align with RBI Master Direction (sample testing of 32 onboarding files = 100% compliance)
- Information-system access controls are operating effectively
- ALM gap is within RBI tolerances

Areas requiring attention:
- Related-party transactions with parent are material + require enhanced governance
- Post-disbursement monitoring is reactive rather than proactive
- Concentration of loan book on group customers

## 3. Detailed Findings

### Finding 3.1 (HIGH) — Loan Book Concentration in Group Customers

**Observation:** As of 30 September 2024, **INR 312 crore** of the Company's INR 482 crore loan book (i.e., 64.7%) was extended to customers of the Nimbus group (i.e., customers of Nimbus Technologies India Pvt Ltd — the sister concern). The largest single exposure is INR 184 crore to **Acme Corp Pvt Ltd** for SaaS + software licence financing.

**Risk:** While each loan is individually credit-sound, the concentration on group-customer base creates correlated credit + reputational risk. RBI NBFC Master Direction also imposes single-borrower + group-borrower limits.

**Compliance gap:** Per Master Direction:
- Single-borrower limit: 15% of Tier-1 Capital. Tier-1 Capital is ~INR 184 cr; 15% = INR 27.6 cr.
- **Acme exposure of INR 184 cr is ~6.7× the single-borrower limit** — apparent breach.

**Mitigating factor:** The Acme exposure is structured as a portfolio of multiple loans (INR 38 cr + INR 42 cr + INR 28 cr + INR 76 cr) across different Acme group entities, each within the limit. However, on a "look-through" basis aggregating all Acme-related parties (subsidiaries + group entities), the aggregate exposure may exceed the single-counterparty group limit (40% of Tier-1 = INR 73.6 cr).

**Recommendation:**
(i) Obtain independent legal + RBI opinion on whether the aggregated Acme exposure constitutes a breach.
(ii) If breach confirmed, develop a 6-month run-off plan + RBI disclosure.
(iii) Diversify book away from group-customer concentration over 12-18 months.

**Management response:** Accepted. Legal opinion commissioned (RIA Advisors, Mumbai). Initial diversification target: bring non-group customer book to ≥60% by 31 March 2026.

### Finding 3.2 (HIGH) — Related-Party Transaction Approval

**Observation:** Of the 28 inter-co transactions with parent (Nimbus Technologies Pte Ltd, Singapore) during H1 FY 2024-25, **6 transactions** (aggregate INR 142 crore) did NOT obtain prior approval from the Indian Audit Committee. The transactions appear to be in the ordinary course + on arm's-length terms; however, the Audit Committee charter requires prior approval for related-party transactions above INR 5 crore per transaction.

**Risk:** Governance failure; potential breach of Companies Act 2013 Section 188.

**Recommendation:** Ratify the 6 transactions post-facto at next Audit Committee meeting with detailed documentation; establish stricter pre-clearance workflow.

**Management response:** Accepted. Ratification on agenda for 28 February 2025 Audit Committee meeting. Workflow updated.

### Finding 3.3 (HIGH) — Standard Asset Provisioning

**Observation:** Per RBI's prudential norms for NBFCs, standard-asset provisioning at minimum 0.40% of standard-asset gross balance is required. Our calculation of the Company's standard-asset provision is INR 1.92 cr (0.40% of INR 480 cr) but actual balance-sheet provision is **INR 1.42 cr** — a shortfall of INR 0.50 cr.

**Risk:** Regulatory breach + understated provisioning.

**Recommendation:** Top up the standard-asset provision to INR 1.92 cr at H1 close (or earlier).

**Management response:** Accepted. Additional INR 0.50 cr provision will be made in the H2 FY 2024-25 financial statements.

### Finding 3.4 (MEDIUM) — Post-Disbursement Monitoring Lag

**Observation:** Post-disbursement monitoring (covenant compliance, financial-statement collection, collateral revaluation) for 8 of the 24 sample loans tested was delayed by 30+ days vs SLA.

**Recommendation:** Implement automated tickler-based reminders; assign dedicated post-disbursement officer.

**Management response:** Accepted. Implementation Q4 FY 2024-25.

### Finding 3.5 (MEDIUM) — ALM Cohort Imbalance

**Observation:** The Company's asset-liability maturity profile shows a meaningful negative gap in the 1-3 year bucket (assets maturing < liabilities maturing). Gap is INR (84) crore, ~17% of equity. Within RBI tolerance (25%) but at the upper end.

**Recommendation:** Reduce gap to within 10% via either liability extension (longer-tenor NCDs) or asset shortening.

**Management response:** Accepted; Treasury team analysing options. Target: gap < 12% by H2 close.

### Finding 3.6 (MEDIUM) — Director-Cross-Appointment Conflict Management

**Observation:** Ms. Priya Iyer is a Director — Legal at the Company AND a VP Legal at the sister-concern Nimbus Technologies India Pvt Ltd, AND a Director at Acme Corp Pvt Ltd (largest single counterparty). Conflict-of-interest declarations are on file, but the Audit Committee minutes from the H1 period do not consistently document her recusal from related-party decisions.

**Recommendation:** Strengthen documentation of conflict declarations + recusals in Committee minutes.

**Management response:** Accepted. Minutes template updated.

### Finding 3.7 (MEDIUM) — Stress-Test Update Lag

**Observation:** The Company's stress-test scenarios (per RBI guidelines for NBFCs) were last updated in October 2023. Macro conditions have changed materially in the intervening 12 months.

**Recommendation:** Update stress-test scenarios + present revised results to Risk Committee within 60 days.

**Management response:** Accepted. Risk Committee meeting agenda includes stress-test refresh; target completion 31 March 2025.

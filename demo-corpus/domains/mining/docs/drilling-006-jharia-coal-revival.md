# DRILLING REPORT — JHARIA COAL BLOCK (REVIVAL ASSESSMENT)

**Operator:** Tata Steel Mining Operations
**Block:** Jharia Coal Block — Sealed-off section (originally abandoned 1986 due to underground fire; revival under evaluation)
**Report date:** 30 January 2025
**Prepared by:** CMPDI — Special Studies group

## 1. Scope

Drilling programme to assess revival feasibility of the Sm-IIA seam in the abandoned south-eastern portion of the Jharia coalfield. The original mine was sealed off in **August 1986** due to underground coal fire which has since been largely controlled (subsidence monitoring confirms quiescence since 2018).

**12 holes** drilled between **September 2024 and January 2025**, total drilled length **3,524 m**. Holes drilled through reclaimed surface; cores examined for:
- (a) Remaining coal resource;
- (b) Thermal history of the seam (evidence of past in-situ combustion);
- (c) Roof + floor stability indicators.

## 2. Remaining Resource Estimate

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Remaining tonnage in Sm-IIA | **24 Mt** (revised down from original 1985 estimate of 38 Mt) |
| Mean ash | 26.4 % |
| Mean calorific value | 4,180 kcal/kg (degraded — see §3) |

## 3. Thermal Damage Findings

7 of 12 holes show evidence of past thermal degradation in the seam — coke-formation rims at the seam edges, increased ash content, reduced calorific value. The central 5 holes show relatively intact coal. **The northern 60 hectares are likely too damaged for economic revival; the southern 80 hectares may be viable.**

## 4. Subsidence Monitoring

Surface subsidence monitoring (4 permanent monitoring points installed) shows stability since 2018. Maximum subsidence in past 5 years: 12 mm/year — within acceptable bounds for revival planning.

## 5. Roof + Floor Stability

The shale roof in the southern 80 hectares is intact; the floor (siltstone) is generally stable but shows minor heave in 2 boreholes. Recommended mining method: **room-and-pillar with mechanical bolting**, not longwall (longwall is unsuitable given the residual thermal stress).

## 6. Critical Safety Considerations

> ⚠ **A revival of the Jharia sealed section MUST be preceded by extensive gas surveying.** Methane build-up in sealed sections is a well-documented hazard. The seam should be re-classified as Gassy Category III pending dynamic gas testing.

## 7. Recommendations

- Initiate gas surveying and ventilation feasibility study **before** detailed mine-plan submission.
- Engage with DGMS for advance consultation on the revival approval pathway.
- Plan for substantially elevated PPE + monitoring on any revival operation.

> **Note (added post-report):** The fatal roof collapse at Jharia on 30 July 2025 (incident-005-jharia-fatal-roof-collapse) occurred in a separate, currently-operating section of Jharia — **not** in the sealed-off section covered by this revival report. Nevertheless, the broader Jharia operation has been placed on heightened safety review.

# LEGAL OPINION — INDIA TAX TREATMENT OF CROSS-BORDER SAAS ROYALTIES

**Mehta & Associates LLP**
Express Towers, Nariman Point, Mumbai 400021

**To:** Mr. Robert Sharma, CEO, Acme Corp Pvt Ltd
**From:** Aisha Mehta, Esq., Partner
**Date:** March 20, 2025
**Re:** Tax treatment of SaaS subscription fees paid by Acme to Vertex Industries Inc.

---

## 1. Introduction

You have requested our opinion on the tax treatment under the Income Tax Act, 1961 (as amended) of subscription fees paid by Acme Corp Pvt Ltd ("**Acme**" or "**Customer**") to Vertex Industries Inc. ("**Vertex**" or "**Vendor**"), a U.S.-incorporated company, in respect of access to Vertex's document-intelligence SaaS platform under the Master Services Agreement dated March 15, 2024 (as amended).

## 2. Factual Background

2.1 Acme is an Indian company tax-resident in India under §6(3) of the Income Tax Act.

2.2 Vertex is a U.S. tax-resident, not having a permanent establishment ("PE") in India.

2.3 The contract grants Acme a non-exclusive right to access cloud-hosted software via subscription; the software is not installed on Acme's premises, and Acme does not acquire any copyright or sublicensing rights.

2.4 Annual subscription fees: approximately USD 750,000 per the current Vendor Agreement.

## 3. Issue

Whether the subscription fees paid by Acme to Vertex are characterized as (a) **royalties** taxable under §9(1)(vi) of the Act (and Article 12 of the India-USA DTAA), or (b) **business profits** under Article 7 of the DTAA, which would be exempt from Indian tax in the absence of a PE.

## 4. Analysis

4.1 The Indian Supreme Court in *Engineering Analysis Centre of Excellence Pvt Ltd v. CIT* (2021) 432 ITR 471 (SC) held that payments for the right to use copyrighted software, where no transfer of copyright (or rights in copyright) is involved, do not constitute "royalty" under §9(1)(vi) or Article 12 of the relevant DTAA.

4.2 The CBDT clarification dated April 2021 reaffirms this position for cross-border SaaS arrangements where:
- the customer obtains a non-exclusive right to access;
- no copyright transfer occurs;
- the software is centrally hosted by the licensor.

4.3 In the present case, the Vendor Agreement explicitly provides that "**all right, title, and interest in the Service remains with Licensor**" (Section 5 of license-002-vertex-acme-saas) and grants Acme only access rights. No reverse-engineering, sublicensing, or copyright transfer is contemplated.

## 5. Conclusion

5.1 The subscription fees should be characterized as **business profits** under Article 7 of the India-USA DTAA, not royalties. Consequently:

> (a) **No withholding tax** under §195 of the Act is required, provided Vertex furnishes a valid Form 10F + tax-residency certificate.
> (b) Equalisation Levy under the Finance Act 2020 does **not apply**, as it was eliminated for e-commerce supply of services from 2024.

5.2 We recommend that Acme:
- (a) Obtain and maintain Form 10F + TRC from Vertex annually;
- (b) Disclose this position in its transfer-pricing documentation;
- (c) Re-evaluate this conclusion if the Vendor Agreement is materially amended (e.g., to grant copyright rights or to install software on Acme's premises).

## 6. Limitations

6.1 This opinion is limited to the facts described and to the law as in force on the date of this opinion. Acme should re-consult counsel if any material change in facts or law occurs.

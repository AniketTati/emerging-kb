# LEGAL OPINION — ENFORCEABILITY OF SINGAPORE ARBITRATION AWARDS IN INDIA AND THE UNITED STATES

**Stark Levin & Co**

**To:** Mr. Kenji Tanaka, Managing Director, Nimbus Technologies Pte Ltd
**From:** Jonathan Levin, Esq., Partner
**Date:** August 15, 2025
**Re:** Cross-border enforceability of Singapore-seated SIAC arbitral awards

---

## 1. Introduction

You have requested our opinion on the enforceability in India and in the United States of arbitral awards rendered in Singapore-seated SIAC arbitrations. This question is relevant in the context of Nimbus's MSA with Acme Corp Pvt Ltd (Singapore-governed, SIAC arbitration in Singapore), under which Acme is an Indian entity and Nimbus may have U.S. counterparties in the future.

## 2. Summary of Conclusion

2.1 **India.** A Singapore-seated SIAC award is generally enforceable in India under Part II of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act, 1996, as Singapore is a notified "reciprocating territory" under the New York Convention 1958. The narrow grounds for refusal of enforcement under §48 of the 1996 Act apply.

2.2 **United States.** A Singapore-seated SIAC award is enforceable in the United States under Chapter 2 of the Federal Arbitration Act (9 U.S.C. §§201-208), which implements the New York Convention. Refusal grounds are narrow and parallel those in India.

## 3. India — Detailed Analysis

3.1 Singapore is a Convention country notified by the Government of India under §44 of the Arbitration and Conciliation Act.

3.2 To enforce in India, the award-holder must file an application before the High Court having jurisdiction over the assets/respondent, accompanied by:
- (a) original or duly authenticated copy of the award;
- (b) original or duly authenticated copy of the arbitration agreement;
- (c) such evidence as may be necessary to prove the award is a foreign award.

3.3 The Supreme Court in *Vijay Karia v. Prysmian Cavi e Sistemi SRL* (2020) 11 SCC 1 has narrowed §48 grounds for refusal — public-policy refusal is reserved for fundamental policy of Indian law, justice/morality, or fraud.

3.4 Recent decisions (e.g., *NTT Docomo v. Tata Sons*) have streamlined enforcement timelines, although court backlog remains a practical concern.

## 4. United States — Detailed Analysis

4.1 Enforcement under 9 U.S.C. §207. Petition filed within three (3) years of the award becoming binding.

4.2 Refusal grounds are limited to those in Article V of the New York Convention: incapacity, lack of proper notice, scope of submission, composition of tribunal, award not yet binding, subject-matter not arbitrable, or public policy.

4.3 U.S. courts apply a strong presumption in favor of enforcement; public-policy defenses succeed only in narrow circumstances.

## 5. Practical Recommendations

5.1 In contracts with Indian or U.S. counterparties:
- (a) Ensure the arbitration clause is unambiguous (seat, governing law of the contract, language, number of arbitrators).
- (b) Maintain original signed contracts in secure storage for future enforcement actions.

5.2 In the Acme MSA, the clause meets standard enforceability requirements.

## 6. Limitations

6.1 This opinion is limited to facts disclosed and law as of August 15, 2025. Indian and U.S. case law in this area evolves; periodic review recommended.
